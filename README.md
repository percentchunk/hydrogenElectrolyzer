# Compact Water Electrolysis Demonstrator

A compact, modular water electrolysis demonstrator inspired by the small “arc reactor” aesthetic.

The project is built around a transparent cylindrical chamber, removable internal electrode cartridge, external structural tie rods, dedicated fill/drain and vent ports, and a custom upright PLA display stand.

The mechanical design was created in Onshape and has already been validated with a full-size 3D-printed fit prototype. The prototype confirmed that the main geometry, stand fit, cap alignment, cartridge clearance, and overall proportions work as intended.

## Design Overview

### Main Chamber

- Clear cylindrical body
- 70 mm outer diameter
- 66 mm inner diameter
- 60 mm chamber length
- Removable top and bottom caps
- Six external structural tie rods around the chamber

### Electrode Cartridge

- Removable internal cartridge
- 10 stainless-steel disks
- Two stainless-steel conductive rods
- Cartridge designed to slide out with the top section
- Disk spacing kept modular so the spacer design can be changed later

### Top Cap

- Dedicated fill/drain port
- Separate atmospheric vent
- Removable design for chamber access

### Bottom Cap

- Positive and negative terminal locations
- Internal rods aligned with the terminal positions
- Electrical isolation features planned around the pass-throughs

### Display Stand

- Printed in PLA
- Upright U-shaped cradle
- Sized around the 90 mm cap
- 92 mm cradle inner diameter
- Reinforced with support ribs and fillets
- Designed to support the main body without loading the terminals

---

## Physical Prototype

A full-size PLA prototype was printed before ordering the final metal hardware.

The prototype was used to verify:

- overall scale
- stand fit
- cap geometry
- cartridge clearance
- terminal clearance
- tie-rod alignment
- assembly proportions

The printed cap measured approximately 89.5 mm compared with the 90 mm CAD dimension, which was close enough to validate the overall mechanical layout.

The prototype is currently held together with temporary printed/placeholding hardware rather than the final metal threaded rods and nuts.
